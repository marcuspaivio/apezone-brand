The question

Which typeface carries everything that isn’t the logo — song titles, gig posters, the website, the press kit — without losing to a logo that is being redrawn deliberately heavy?

Constraints set before looking: free and open-licensed, self-hosted (no subscription, no dependence on anyone’s employer), real weight, a road-sign register without literally being the German traffic typeface, numbers that line up in columns, and flawless ä ö Ä Ö.

The answer

Barlow. One family for everything — its heaviest weight for display, its regular weight for reading, two steps between for headings and small text. Approved by Marcus 2026-08-01.

Ten families were measured; the full working record is brand/typography.md. Unusually for these reports, what you see below is not a concept — the real font files are embedded in this page, so every line of the specimen is the actual deliverable.

BESIDE THE LOGO

The test that decided it

The logo is being redrawn at double its 2013 weight — stroke thickness 24% of the capital height. A typeface next to it has to hold its own without shouting over it. That is a measurable property, so it was measured: the thickness of each candidate’s heaviest H, read from the font files themselves.

Barlow Black measures 26.9% — near enough the logo’s own weight that the two read as kin, and inside the 22–26% band the logo specification itself allows. Every other candidate was either clearly thinner or clearly heavier.

Also excluded without measuring: Bebas Neue and Anton — the two default “heavy poster” fonts of the last decade. Both are competent; both are so widely used that they read as template. No measurement answers that objection, so none was run. The literal German traffic face (DIN) has no sufficiently heavy open version, so it fell out on weight before licensing was even a question.

WHY BARLOW

Five reasons, one honest weakness

  • It matches the logo’s weight instead of fighting it. 26.9% against 24% — the closest in the field. The runner-up (Archivo, 32.1%) commands attention on its own, but next to the wordmark it competes where Barlow defers.
  • The signage register is documented, not imagined. Barlow’s own description: drawn from the visual style of California’s public signage — car plates, highway signs, buses, trains. The identity’s poster grammar was prototyped on a traffic typeface as a placeholder; Barlow keeps that voice without copying the traffic face.
  • Numbers line up. Gig lists, the discography and 1997–2027 all need digits in columns — verified in the font’s feature tables, shown live in the specimen above.
  • Finnish survives. All the dotted capitals are properly drawn and sit correctly at display sizes — not a given, and expensive to discover late.
  • Free, open, self-hosted. SIL Open Font License 1.1, verified from the licence file that ships with the fonts — it explicitly permits self-hosting, web conversion and logo use. Four files, ~127 KB, no subscription, nothing loads from Google.
The honest weakness

Barlow’s regular weight is the lightest text face that was shortlisted. At website body size (16px, light-on-dark) it holds comfortably; below 14px it starts to thin. The rule written into the system: anything smaller steps up one weight. If the website ends up dominated by very small text, the runner-up’s sturdier regular is worth revisiting before Phase 4 builds — swapping is a one-session job and nothing else moves.

HONEST LIMITS

What this decision cannot claim

  • Judged on screens only. Nothing has been seen on paper, at poster size, or on a real phone. The first printed proof may still move the small-size rules.
  • The logo in this page is a weight proxy, not the final drawing — see the caption above. The type-beside-mark judgement will be re-checked against the real master when 2.2 produces it.
  • Thickness was measured on the capital H at several heights and taken as the family’s figure; diagonals and bars differ, in the logo as well as in the fonts.
  • One person’s judgement. The measurements are arithmetic; the choice between the two finalists was Marcus’s call, and the runner-up is recorded with its costs so the band can disagree with an informed one.
