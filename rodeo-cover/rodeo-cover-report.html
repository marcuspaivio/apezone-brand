01

Where this sits

After the illustrated register and Böbbis character rounds had established the language, Rodeo is the first attempt to use it for one specific song. The lyrics turn a celebrated young football hero into the centre of a public disgrace: a triumphant arena and a private catastrophe at the same time. The cover makes the spectacle the subject rather than illustrating the sexual detail literally.

Marcus approved the underlying composition first. A dark night arena, dirty floodlights, a packed crowd, status debris in the air and the fallen golden boy on the floor. Böbbis is one actor inside the event, carrying the approved narrow ape jaw and the guitar constant.

02

The approved picture before treatment

This is band-approved digital cover artwork, not licensed print or merchandise artwork. The source is AI-generated. No print test, physical proof or generation-rights clearance has happened.

03

The treatment that was wrong

The first finishing pass globally posterised and recoloured the image into a brown-and-blue system. Marcus stopped it: “I want the same treatment as we did with the posters. Not this.” The important distinction is now explicit. The picture remains the colour source. A quantised copy only identifies a small middle-tone region for dots; it never replaces the approved artwork.

The dot screen covers 3.9% of the square. It is a local printing register, not a filter over the whole frame.

04

The wordmark belongs to this picture

Marcus made the next rule directly: “The wordmark does not always need to be blue either. It should always take its inspiration from the image. So it fits in.” The letterforms still come from the shipped master. What changes is their treatment: this cover samples the dirty amber arena lights and builds the deep-block wordmark from those colours.

The result clears both existing mark gates: 8.19:1 keyline contrast and 0.947 enclosure around the ring. A gate proves legibility, not taste; Marcus's approval proves the latter for this cover.

05

RODEO became part of the arena

A large floating song title was readable but wrong. It competed with the image and had no reason to be there. The arena already contained the right surface: its scoreboard.

The final title is not a scoreboard-looking font. It is five fixed 5×7 lamp glyphs inside a 31×9 field, with active bulbs, dim inactive bulbs and warm bloom. The field is mapped into the board's trapezoid so every lamp follows the illustrated perspective.

The first placement was mathematically centred and visibly off. Marcus guided the last corrections by eye: centre it in the board, lift the low right side to the top-edge skew, then move the whole matrix in small increments. Optical alignment finished what geometry started.

The approved cover

Marcus after the final optical move: “That's perfect.”

07

What carries forward

  • Preserve an approved picture's colours; treatment is not permission to recolour it.
  • Use the retro-print dots locally, never as a whole-frame filter.
  • Let each image choose the wordmark colour while the master keeps the letterforms.
  • Give the song title a role distinct from the band name.
  • Prefer an in-world title surface. Build its actual visual language and follow its perspective.
  • Judge final placement optically at full size and at streaming-thumbnail size.
  • Keep Böbbis's identity features and guitar clear; the illustrated jaw does not alter the logo.
08

Method, limits and adjudication

One source image was generated. The tool exposed no credit cost, so none is claimed. Everything after that — colour preservation, dots, wordmark treatment, lamp letters, perspective and exports — is deterministic and rebuildable from build.py. No generated lettering survives.

Nothing later overrules the result. It applies the standing illustrated register, the approved narrow illustrated jaw and free colour for unbounded artwork. The shipped logo is untouched.

Band approval was confirmed on 31 August 2026. Still open: print proofing, platform upload, narrative/legal review and the generation licence for print or merchandise. Creative approval does not clear those production gates.
