The question

Does a complete ape skull — with the top of the head and a lower jaw — beat the front-only fragment that round F chose?

Round F answered its question by hand: its verdict records “generation budget used: 0”, and no complete skull had ever been designed and tested in any round. This round closed that gap with the generators: twenty-three skulls, anchored on the band’s own drawings, measured against the incumbent at real pixel sizes.

The answer

No. The fragment holds. Every complete skull renders its face 1.5–1.76× smaller in the same space, the ape cue dies first at small sizes, and the jaw never once arrived calm. Confirming the incumbent with better evidence was a named acceptable outcome of the brief, and it is the outcome.

Confirmed by Marcus the same day, reviewing the published comparison: the front-only fragment stays the detailed form, the small sizes stay with the already-decided small skull — “this round made that very clear.”

Not artwork

Everything on this page is a generated concept — images made to settle a shape question. Nothing here is the logo, nothing here ships, and the two vector traces discussed below are traces, not drawings. The real vector master is still a separate, unstarted job.

THE ASK

The stencil, completed — crown and jaw

Mid-round, Marcus pasted in the drawing the whole skull lineage descends from — the r1e stencil — and asked for a version of this with a jaw and a proper crown. Six completions were generated with the stencil itself as the reference: two faithful to its register, two with the eye sockets levelled, two with the mouth ordered shut.

Where this lands

The completed stencil takes the same slot round F gave the horns: a one-off poster or shirt in the loud register the direction already licenses for merch — chosen knowingly, one piece at a time. Not the identity: the snarl is exactly the announced menace the platform excludes, and on the dark ground it fills in to noise below 48 px, as the 2013 drawing always did.

Adopting it as identity would mean reopening the design direction deliberately — a repositioning, not a pick.

THE FIELD

Twenty-three generations, two routes

Both routes ran: Seedream-class raster, reference-guided on the parsed Skull.ai and round B’s detailed jawless drawing, and Recraft producing vector directly (it takes no references — a real asymmetry, recorded rather than hidden). Four jaw treatments were varied deliberately: mouth closed, partial mandible, jaw as one solid mass, tooth row suppressed to a band.

Twenty-three of a budgeted twenty-five were used, stopped on the brief’s own rule: three jaw strategies and both routes had shown their pattern, and the marginal generation was buying variance rather than information.

VERDICT

Three findings, one decision

1 · The jaw refuses to come back calm

Three strategies across twenty-three generations — sealed mouth, jaw as one mass, suppressed tooth row — and not one calm closed mandible. The models either drop the jaw entirely (the band’s own jawless configuration is that strong in the references) or return it snarling or cute. The grinning tooth row — the generic-skull tell the jawless idea exists to avoid — was not merely a risk. It was the only jaw on offer. Round B’s no-mandible rule was not a local finding about one drawing; it survives generation at scale.

2 · Integral crowns do not read as headgear

The one genuinely new positive result. Round F’s drawn crowns kept reading as hood, cowl, brim or helmet; no skull generated whole ever did. The headgear read comes from adding a lid to an existing face-plate, not from craniums as such. If a crowned skull is ever wanted for large-format work, it should be made whole — never assembled upward from the fragment.

3 · The crest dies at 32 px, every time

Whatever a crown claims at poster size, no generated crest survived reduction. A complete skull buys the ape cue where it isn’t needed and loses it where it is. The small-size skull — which carries its crest as a solid mass, verified in rounds D and F — keeps that job, unchanged.

This is the project’s recurring conclusion at its highest resolution yet: generation informs, the band’s own material wins — round 1 said it, round D confirmed it for the small skull, and round G now closes it for the whole skull.

PROVENANCE

Checked, with the limit stated

  • The space is crowded, reconfirmed: 310+ ape-skull stock illustrations on iStock, 905 on Shutterstock, ready-made “ape skull logo” templates sold on Freepik, and at least one band (Bison B.C., Metal Blade) selling a gorilla-skull shirt. A model generating “gorilla skull logo” samples exactly this pool.
  • Visual sanity check: no candidate resembles the Bison B.C. mark or the template found.
  • The stencil completions descend from the band’s own drawing. On the standing rule — the candidate derived from the band’s own artwork wins on provenance alone — the completed stencils beat the from-scratch skulls before aesthetics enter into it.
What was not checked

No reverse-image search was run against any candidate — the tooling is not available in these sessions. Originality of the from-scratch candidates is therefore unverified and stays unverified. Same limitation as round D, recorded rather than glossed; it matters only if a generated mark is ever adopted, and none is.

WHAT THIS CHANGES

One finding for future large-format work Crowned skulls read as bone only when made whole. Never assemble a crown onto the fragment.
HONEST LIMITS

What this round cannot tell you

  • All size claims are exact-pixel renders inspected magnified — a good proxy, not a real browser tab, a phone, or paper. The real-tab check is still owed to the drawing task, unchanged.
  • The “cute” and “generic” reads at 24–32px are one person’s reads of a magnified render. The strips above are built so a cold viewer can disagree.
  • The two routes are not symmetric: the raster route saw the band’s reference drawings, the direct-vector route cannot take references. Recraft’s worse register drift may partly be that, not the model.
  • No reverse-image search on any candidate. See provenance above.
  • Twenty-three of twenty-five budgeted generations used. Stopped on the stopping rule, not on exhaustion.
