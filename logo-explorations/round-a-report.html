ROUND 0

Open the vectors first

The brief said to open the archive vectors before generating anything. Doing it changed the rest of the session. The .ai files come in two incompatible formats and neither opens here — no Illustrator, no Ghostscript, no cairo — so they were parsed directly: legacy Illustrator 8 PostScript for the 2013 components, inflated PDF content streams for the modern ones, then rendered with a hand-written bézier flattener and even-odd scanline fill.

Both key assets came out clean. The archive’s vector material is now format-independent and can be handed to a designer as SVG rather than as 13-year-old .ai.

ROUND 1

The generators lose

Six skull directions, six prompts, one image each. Every one drifted from cranium to gorilla face — sockets drawn as angry eyes, brow as a scowl, jaw as a beard. Four of the six turned the nasal opening into a literal heart shape.

The band’s own 2013 drawing beats all of them at the actual job. That settles the redraw-or-generate question, and it is the opposite of what a generation round is supposed to conclude.

Do not present 1b as archival

The engraving invented a plate caption — Fig. 46. Gorillae caput. — and a plate number. It looks like a historical natural-history plate and is not one. The Latin comes off before this goes anywhere near a public surface.

ROUND 2

How much has to come out

With the geometry in hand, “is it too detailed?” stops being a judgement and becomes arithmetic. Ink area per subpath, as a share of the whole:

SubpathShare of inkWhat it is
162.6%the main silhouette
211.6% 
3–620.8%sockets, muzzle, cheek mass
7–53~5%forty-seven paths sharing this between them

So the drawing is six shapes plus forty-seven decorations — and the decorations carry almost all the complexity and almost none of the legibility. Removing them makes the mark better: heavier, calmer, more menacing.

Two things mechanical reduction gets wrong and a person must fix: the nasal opening collapses into a heart or spade and wants to be a narrow vertical slot; the teeth become rounded blocks and want to be one flat band interrupted only by the canines.

Why 16px is arithmetic, not craft

The full cranium stacks six horizontal bands — crest, brow, sockets, cheek flare, muzzle, teeth. Six bands into sixteen pixels is 2.7px each. No amount of path-dropping fixes that.

Cropping does. Keeping the upper 60% — crest, brow, both sockets, the top of the flare — reduces to three bands and comes out clean. Crop further, to 48%, and it reads as goggles. The cliff is sharp and worth knowing where it is.

ROUND 3

Lockups, and the disagreement

Six variants, composed from the real 2013 glyph outlines. Two elements do not exist in the archive and were constructed to match the measured letterforms — a plain ring O, and a crossbar for the A. Measured spec to carry into task 2.3: cap height 131.3, stem 15.8 — a 12.0% stroke.

Direction 2 — skull in the O

What task 2.1 recommends, and what the band arrived at independently twice — the 2013 cover and the 2015 site. A real signal, not to be waved away.

  • Genuinely distinctive in a way Direction 1 is not
  • Solves mark-versus-wordmark in one move
  • Busier at every size; floor rises to 220px
  • Adding 1997–2027 makes three elements compete
Two smaller findings

The crossbar earns its place. The 2013 A is a bare chevron, so the word can read ΛPEZ⊙NE. With no tagline, the wordmark carries the identity alone and cannot afford to be misread. A legibility argument, not a taste one — but it is thirteen years of Marcus’s letterform, so it is his call.

The letterspacing is uneven. Bounding-box gaps run 84.3 · 98.8 · 91.7 · 67.7 · 60.8 · 81.3 — a 62% spread in a wordmark whose whole idea is even spacing. Round letters legitimately sit tighter, so this is flagged for optical respacing rather than asserted as wrong.

ROUND 4

Colour — red loses

Decision 6 required red to earn its place against at least two non-red candidates. Three were tested. Red lost on measurement.

Red manages 2.48:1 against the near-black ground the identity actually lives on — it cannot carry a link, a date, or any emphasis. And muted enough to satisfy decision 6, it stops being warmth. A red saturated enough to do the job is the 2011 red.

The winner is one warm hue at two values, mirroring the neutrals’ own structure. The whole palette is therefore two hues: 201° cold, 40° warm.

One caveat on the premise, not the result

This round started from the 2015 palette because the brief said to. The brief has since been revised — the 2015 era is now to be treated as a themed one-off, and its palette as one candidate among several rather than the default.

That splits the result. The accent contest still stands: four candidates, run on measured contrast, red beaten on its own merits. The neutrals do not — they were derived from the 2015 ramp without alternatives being tested. If the revised brief is to be honoured, the neutral ramp should be re-run against at least one warm-neutral and one true-neutral alternative before 2.6 locks it.

Before anything is printed

The CMYK values in colour.md are unmanaged conversions with no profile and no proof. Gold-family colours reproduce badly in four-colour process, so get a spot match for Aged Brass before merch or print. No Pantone references have been invented.

Also: Ink against Slate is 1.26:1. They vanish into each other on cheap panels and after social recompression — never use that step alone to carry structure.

ROUND 5

Feed it the real geometry

Round 1 described the skull in words and got mascots. Round 5 handed the model the band’s own reduced geometry as an image reference and asked for specific edits — and both fixes mechanical reduction could not make came out right: the nasal opening became a narrow slot, and the teeth became one band interrupted by the canines.

If any further generation happens on this project, feed it the archive geometry. Text-only prompting for this mark wastes credits.

Nothing was auto-traced to SVG. Tracing a generated raster would produce a worse vector than the 53 clean fills already in hand — the route to final artwork runs through Skull.ai, not through anything generated here.

PROPOSAL

Three tiers, one geometry

LIMITS

What this is not

  • None of this is deliverable artwork. Every image is either a generated concept or a mechanical transformation of the archive geometry. The final mark still has to be drawn by hand.
  • The lockups are real 2013 outlines re-composed by script. The ring O and the crossbar are constructed approximations, not drawn letterforms.
  • The 1997–2027 in round 3 came out as seven-segment digits — a placeholder from having no typeface yet. Judge its size and position; ignore its shapes.
  • The 16px crop reads as a heavy-browed skull, not specifically as an ape. An acceptable trade at that size, but a deliberate one.
  • Nothing here has been seen at physical size, on paper, or on a phone. The small-size tests are renders at nominal pixel sizes — a good proxy, not the same thing.
