Pending band review

These are explorations, not proposals — nobody has asked for the mark to be changed and nothing here is a brand asset. The band has not seen any of it.

Wordmark — the bold set

Round two. Round one only painted the surface; this set splits the master into its seven letters and lets arrangement move too — arcs, stagger, explosions, tears, perspective. No point moves inside a letter: A P E Z O N E are the master's own outlines, placed differently. That is the line this set does not cross.

Poster, merch and end-card ideas — not brand assets. Nearly all of it breaks assets/logos/USAGE.md, and Riveted breaks colour.md §5 rule 3 as well by making Steel the mark. Nothing is written to assets/. There was no image generation involved and none was available: every pixel here is drawn by build_bold.py from the vector master.
The drips, icicles and rivets are not placed by eye. For each letter the baseline runs — the pairs of coordinates sitting on y = -465.55 — are read out of the path data, so something can only hang where the letter genuinely touches the baseline. Runs found per letter: A 2, P 1, E 1, Z 1, O 1, N 2, E 1. The O has none, because its bottom is a cubic curve that never lands exactly on the line, and it is special-cased to its own centre — the single hand-placed number in this file.

The set

Arch

Arrangement arch.svg

The oldest trick in band-logo history and still the best one: every letter set on a 1500-unit circle, each rotated by its own arc position so the spacing along the curve is the spacing the master already had. Brass emboss over the top. This is the front of a shirt.

HoldsThe arch is a shape, not a detail — it survives to any size the letters themselves survive to.

Effect floor 392 px — bevel blur radius 4.5 units, needs 1.5 px

Stomp

Arrangement stomp.svg

Every letter knocked off the baseline in alternation, rotated a few degrees, and given twenty-two steps of extrusion. Nothing is redrawn — it is the master's own seven letters, thrown down a flight of stairs.

HoldsDepth is mass and the stagger is gross geometry, so both survive the way down. The word gets harder to read as a *word* before it gets harder to see.

Effect floor 107 px — extrusion depth 22 units, needs 2 px

Blowout

Arrangement blowout.svg

The word coming apart from the middle out — each letter thrown radially by a fifth of its distance from centre, with seven ghost copies smeared back along its own path. Reading order survives because nothing overtakes its neighbour.

SoftensThe ghosts merge into a haze below about 200 px and the gaps start reading as broken spacing rather than as motion.

Effect floor 196 px — ghost step 9 units, needs 1.5 px

Dub echo

Arrangement dub-echo.svg

Nine repeats of the whole word marching right into the dark, brass decaying to Ink, the readable one at the front. A delay line you can look at — and the only treatment here that gets more interesting the more of the surface you give it.

HoldsThe front copy is the untouched master at full contrast; everything behind it is atmosphere. Worst case small is a slightly dirty edge.

Effect floor 90 px — echo step 26 units, needs 2 px

Arena

Arrangement arena.svg

Thirty-four copies scaled toward a vanishing point 620 units below the baseline, so the extrusion converges instead of stepping — the word standing on a stage and receding into it. Chrome face, because at this point why not.

SoftensThe converging steps compress to a solid dark mass small, which reads as a heavy shadow rather than as depth. Still legible.

Effect floor 735 px — first step offset 1.6 units, needs 1 px

Wrecked

Arrangement wrecked.svg

Torn straight across at 54% of the cap height along a jagged seam, the halves pulled apart and rotated against each other. Every letterform is still whole — the tear is a clip, not a cut — and the word still reads, which is the surprise.

HoldsA tear is gross geometry. Both halves keep full Bone contrast and the seam is the last thing to go, not the first.

Effect floor 136 px — seam jitter 13 units, needs 1.5 px

Alternating

Arrangement alternating.svg

The cheapest bold move available and the only one on this page that would survive a screen printer's phone call: alternate letters in Lamp Amber and Bone, keyed with an Ink keyline. Nothing moves, nothing bevels, two inks total.

HoldsTwo flat inks and the master's own geometry — the only treatment in either set with no effect to lose, so it is identical at 96 px and at a metre wide. Arch, Stomp and Molten also hold; this one holds for free.

Effect floor 588 px — keyline 2 units, needs 1 px

Molten

Surface molten.svg

Poured brass, still running. 8 drips and 3 falling beads, each one hung from a baseline run read straight out of the path data — so a drip starts only where a letter genuinely touches the baseline. The A gets two, the N gets two, the P gets one under its stem, and the O is the one exception.

SoftensThe drips are the whole idea and they are the first thing to go: below the floor opposite they merge into a fringe under the word.

Effect floor 140 px — drip width 21 units, needs 2.5 px

Frostbite

Surface frostbite.svg

Frozen: a pale ice ramp, 7 fracture lines running top to bottom and clipped inside the letters, and icicles hung off the same derived baseline runs the drips use. All of it sits inside the palette's 201° — this is the cold treatment that does not need an off-brand colour.

SoftensThe cracks vanish first and the icicles go to fuzz; what is left is a pale wordmark, which is fine but is no longer ice.

Effect floor 490 px — fracture stroke 2.4 units, needs 1 px

Riveted

Surface riveted.svg

Fabricated rather than drawn: hard-bevelled Steel plate with 30 rivets punched through it, set on the derived foot runs and mirrored up at each letter's own cap line. Steel is a legal ground and an illegal mark, so this one is doubly out of bounds and worth looking at anyway.

FailsRivets are 11 units across and the bevel is tighter still. Below the floor opposite this is a flat grey wordmark with a case of measles.

Effect floor 315 px — rivet diameter 11.2 units, needs 3 px

Wet neon

Surface wet-neon.svg

The sign, and the pavement under it. The tube is mirrored below the baseline, squashed to 62%, blurred and faded into a reflection, with a wet sheen wiped across it. Bar door at two in the morning — the loudest thing in either set.

SoftensSame as round one's neon, and the reflection makes it worse small: two hazy bands instead of one. It wants 240 px and up.

Effect floor 294 px — tube core 8 units, needs 2 px

Burn-in

Surface burn-in.svg

A dying tube: the word warped by low-frequency displacement, bloomed until the highlights clip, split into a cold and a brass phosphor, then striped and hit with a flyback line. Round one's Signal loss was a glitch; this one is a fault.

FailsBloom plus a 7-unit phosphor stripe means everything below the floor opposite is a glowing smear. It is a full-bleed end card, nothing smaller.

Effect floor 504 px — phosphor stripe 7 units, needs 3 px

Reduction — all twelve at 130 px

Bolder is mostly worse down here, and two of them are gone entirely. That is the trade, stated rather than hidden: these are surfaces for large formats.
