Explorations, not proposals

These are explorations, not proposals — nobody has asked for the mark to be changed and nothing here is a brand asset. The band’s 2026‑08‑30 review covered the settled identity and the illustrated work; it did not cover these.

Wordmark — the generated round

Round three of the wordmark explorations, and the first with an image generator connected. Ten candidates, 760 credits, and a single conclusion: none of them is a treatment of the Apezone wordmark. Every one is a mood image, and the measurement that says so is below.

Read this before looking at the pictures. Several of these are genuinely beautiful, and beauty is not the question this round asked. The question was whether a generator can dress the mark without redrawing it. It cannot, at least not through these tools: all ten candidates moved the letterforms, and the two that came closest missed on a 3% squeeze and an IoU of 0.573 against 0.90. A concept is never finished artwork, and a generated wordmark looks finished when it is not even the right word.
These are not brand assets. Like rounds one and two, they break assets/logos/USAGE.md — “no effects: no bevel, glow, gradient, drop shadow, outline stroke” — deliberately, and nothing here proposes amending the mark. USAGE.md rule 1 still describes reality: the shipped mark is one colour, two states. Nothing is written to assets/.

The licence, read 2026-08-11

Checked before a credit was spent, because it decides whether the round can produce anything shippable. The answer moved, and in the permissive direction. Magnific's AI content and copyright page now states plainly:

“AI-generated content can serve as a central element in your creative projects — there is no main vs secondary element restriction for AI-generated content like there is for stock resources.”

Its comparison table gives Main element rule → AI-generated content: “No restriction — can be used as main element commercially.” The secondary-element rule on the merchandise page governs stock resources, and the Merchandise Licence exists to lift that — which is why AI is listed there as not applicable rather than as forbidden. This repo's 2026-08-03 reading in brand/large-format/FINDINGS.md §4.2 read the exclusion as a prohibition. It is the absence of a restriction AI was never under.

Neither page carries a last-updated date, so whether the terms changed or were misread in August cannot be determined from the pages themselves. What is unchanged and still bites: AI output is excluded from Magnific's indemnification entirely — stock on this Premium plan carries €10,000 per claim, AI carries nothing — copyright ownership is “subject to ongoing legal developments”, and trademark is not addressed anywhere in either document. Registration is a lawyer's question, and this page is not answering it.

The correction is recorded, not applied. Fourteen files in this repo carry the superseded conclusion — the two most load-bearing now carry a dated pointer, and the other twelve are untouched, because they are other rounds' conclusions and three of them are builders for reports this round does not own. FINDINGS.md §1.4 lists every one. Anyone acting on it should know the replacement argument is indemnification rather than licence, which makes generated merch uninsured rather than prohibited — a decision, not a find-and-replace.

One mechanism explains most of it

Relight returns a 4:1 frame whatever it is given, and takes the difference out of the input. The wordmark is 7.43:1. Wave one lost two letters off Chrome and squeezed Brass emboss 21% narrower; wave two padded every source onto a 4:1 plate in its own ground so the tool would have nothing to take. It helped — Chrome went from five letters to seven, Brass from a 21% squeeze to 2.5% — and it did not save anything. Arena got worse, squeezed 38% and merged to three letters. The tool re-crops to the subject regardless of the frame it was handed.

The second size axis, and why this round has no effect floor

Rounds one and two compute this exactly: every effect is named and specified in artwork units, so it shrinks at the letterforms' rate and the floor is arithmetic. A generated plate has no units to divide. Two attempts at a substitute are recorded in measure.py, both discarded — the first reported a floor of 8,192 px for the snapped Molten because it was measuring dither dots. The second produced the answer instead of a number: measured across a ladder from 1,200 px to 96 px, the luminance spread inside the ink barely moves.

The palette snap

The step that turns a generated picture into a brand asset: brand/large-format/palette_map.py, every pixel to its nearest of five values in CIELAB with an ordered dither. Raw generated gold drifts orange every time — the shard fields measured 0.39–2.27% red before the snap, and Arena unpadded came in at 2.277%, at the very top of that range.

It works better than expected. Five values cannot describe a specular highlight, and the prediction going in — from colour.md §5 rule 3, and from Marcus declining the skull's third colour state in August — was that a lit metal wordmark would break. It does not: the highlights collapse onto Bone and read as hard screen-printed gleam rather than as broken metal, and the plate stays palette-exact with zero stray pixels.

And it removes exactly what the generator was hired to add. The snapped plate is a good-looking flat gold poster wordmark. It is no longer a photograph of metal, which was the entire reason to spend a credit on it. That is the round's argument against generation in a single image.

The verdict

The incumbents hold. Round one's Brass emboss and round two's Molten are still the best treatments of this wordmark, and they are still drawn. Generation produced four genuinely superior materials — the Chrome plate is better metal than any SVG filter will manage, and the letterpress is a physical process no filter can fake at all — attached to letterforms that are not Apezone's.

Rounds D, F and G all ended “the incumbent holds” and that was the right answer each time. It is the right answer here too, with one thing this round adds that the others did not: the failure is mechanical, not aesthetic. Nothing about the prompts, the lights or the budget caused it. A tool that reframes to a fixed aspect and redraws type as a side effect cannot be pointed at a 7.43:1 wordmark whose O carries a 3.5% optical correction, and no amount of credits changes that.

What the round leaves behind that is worth keeping: the gate, which is reusable on any future generated candidate; the rasteriser, which this repo did not have and which any browser-driven work will want again; the licence correction, which affects task 3.6 rather than this round; and the letterpress plate, which is a good mood image for a sleeve back and should be labelled as one forever.
