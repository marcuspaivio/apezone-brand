Explorations, not proposals

These are explorations, not proposals — nobody has asked for the mark to be changed and nothing here is a brand asset. The band’s 2026‑08‑30 review covered the settled identity and the illustrated work; it did not cover these.

Wordmark — surface treatments

Fourteen dressings of the same mark. The path is brand/logo-build/masters/wordmark-master.svg verbatim in every one — nothing redrawn, respaced or stretched — so the only variable is surface.

These are not brand assets. Most of them break assets/logos/USAGE.md — "no effects: no bevel, glow, gradient, drop shadow, outline stroke" — and that is the point of the exercise, not an oversight. Colours are drawn from brand/colour.md where the effect allows it; Chrome, Liquid and Cold glow need values off the palette to describe a lit surface at all, which is the same finding task 3.2 hit with the skull. Nothing here is written to assets/.

Small sizes, at a glance

Two different questions, and they have different answers. Reads is whether the word is still a word. Effect floor is the width below which the treatment stops being itself — computed from the feature size, not judged by eye: every effect is specified in artwork units, so it shrinks at exactly the rate the letterforms do.

The set

Dusk

Flat dusk.svg

A single vertical ramp, Bone falling to Steel. The tamest thing in this set — no bevel, no light source, nothing to break. Closest to something the guide could actually be talked into.

HoldsNo fine feature to lose. At 96 px the ramp collapses to a flat mid-Bone, which is the master.

Chrome

Metal chrome.svg

The full 1984 treatment: a horizon line across the middle of the cap height, sky above, ground below, dark keyline holding it all in. Loud, and it knows it.

SoftensLegible at 96 px but no longer chrome — the horizon band lands mid-stem and reads as a hollow rather than a reflection.

Effect floor 181 px — outer keyline 6.5 units, needs 1 px

Brass emboss

Metal brass-emboss.svg

Aged Brass raised off the ground, lit from the upper left by a distant light — specular highlight on the top-left edges, the fill darkening away from it. A cast plaque rather than a chrome sticker.

HoldsThe best of the metals small: the bevel goes but the fill is solid gold and the silhouette is untouched, so it degrades to a clean brass wordmark instead of to mush.

Effect floor 392 px — bevel blur radius 4.5 units, needs 1.5 px

Gold foil

Metal gold-foil.svg

Hot-foil stamp: a raking brass-to-amber ramp across the word, brushed with anisotropic noise — long horizontally, tight vertically — so it catches like rolled foil rather than sitting flat. The streaks are drawn in artwork units, so they shrink with the mark.

HoldsReads at any size, but stops being foil early — below the floor opposite it is simply a gold gradient.

Effect floor 141 px — brush-streak pitch 16.7 units, needs 2 px

Liquid

Metal liquid.svg

Mercury: the same chrome idea with the horizon pushed low and the surface warped, so it reads as something poured rather than something stamped. Displacement is kept under the stem width, so the letters do not actually deform.

SoftensLegible small, but the highlights bloom together and it stops reading as liquid — it just looks like a slightly damaged chrome.

Effect floor 196 px — displacement scale 9 units, needs 1.5 px

Chisel

Cut chisel.svg

Bone cut into stone. Tight blur radius and a high specular exponent, so the bevel is a hard facet instead of a soft dome — the same language as the engraved skull plate, applied to type.

SoftensThe weakest small performer that still reads. The diffuse pass dims the fill below Bone and there is no keyline, so at 96 px it goes grey against Ink rather than crisp.

Effect floor 678 px — facet blur radius 2.6 units, needs 1.5 px

Deboss

Cut deboss.svg

The one light-ground treatment: the word pressed *into* Paper. Dark inner edge top-left, white inner edge bottom-right, no ink at all. Blind-embossed stock, letterhead, a sleeve back.

FailsThere is no ink here at all — the whole mark is a 3.5-unit shadow. Below the floor opposite, the page is blank. Big and physical only.

Effect floor 336 px — inner-shadow offset 3.5 units, needs 1 px

Punched plate

Cut punched-plate.svg

Inverted: the word is the hole. A brushed Steel panel with APEZONE milled clean through it, the recess showing Ink and the panel casting down into it. The counters become islands — which is where you find out whether the P and the O survive.

HoldsBetter small than it has any right to be, because the plate gives it a hard rectangular ground the letterforms can sit against. The counters close first — watch the P.

Effect floor 168 px — recess shadow offset 7 units, needs 1 px

Extrude

Dimensional extrude.svg

Thirty stacked copies stepping down and right into Steel, Bone face on top. A solid block letter cut from one piece — the depth reads even when the face is small, because the extrusion is mass rather than detail.

HoldsDepth is mass, not detail, so it is the one dimensional treatment that loses nothing on the way down.

Effect floor 87 px — total extrusion depth 27 units, needs 2 px

Neon

Light neon.svg

Lamp Amber bent into tube and traced around the letter contours — including the counters, which is what a real sign maker would do. Four passes: wide haze, soft glow, the tube itself, and a hot white core.

SoftensFine at 180 px and up. At 96 the haze floods the counters and the tube is a hairline — it reads as a warm smudge, not a word.

Effect floor 336 px — tube stroke 7 units, needs 2 px

Cold glow

Light cold-glow.svg

Solid Bone with the light coming off it rather than a tube outline — the mark still reads as a filled wordmark first, lit second. The restrained end of the light family, and the one that would survive a dark website header.

HoldsSolid fill underneath, so the worst case at any size is a slightly hazy wordmark. The safest way to get light into this mark.

Effect floor 353 px — near-glow radius 5 units, needs 1.5 px

Engraved hatch

Screened engraved-hatch.svg

Filled with the same diagonal hairline hatching the large-format skull plate uses to get its material, plus a hairline keyline so the silhouette holds when the hatching stops resolving.

FailsThe worst reducer here. Below the floor opposite the hatch averages to a flat grey, leaving a 3.2-unit keyline to carry the whole mark — and that is under a pixel by 96 px. Poster only.

Effect floor 235 px — hatch pitch 15 units, needs 3 px

Halftone fade

Screened halftone.svg

Solid Bone at the cap line dissolving into a dot screen by the baseline. Print decay, cheap photocopy, a screen that ran out of ink — and the counters stay open because the dissolve only touches the bottom third.

HoldsDegrades gracefully: the dots blur into a soft lower edge rather than to mush, because the top 42% is solid and does the reading.

Effect floor 207 px — dot pitch 17 units, needs 3 px

Signal loss

Screened signal-loss.svg

Channel split into the palette's two hues — cold 201° left, Brass 40° right — then sliced into three bands that don't agree on where the word is, and scanlined. No red, which is the only reason this one is even arguable.

SoftensStill reads at 96 px, but the scanlines alias into moiré and the whole thing looks like a rendering fault — which is either the point or a disaster, depending on where it is placed.

Effect floor 392 px — scanline pitch 9 units, needs 3 px

Reduction — all fourteen at 120 px

The honest test. Each is rendered at 120 px wide on its own intended ground, at the same scale the header of a phone-width site would use. Effects are drawn in artwork units, so they shrink with the mark rather than staying put.
