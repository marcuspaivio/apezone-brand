The question

Does the layout grammar still work once a real logo is put into it?

Round C proposed a way of laying pages out — a line along the top, dates set huge, an activity strip at the foot, exactly one warm accent per surface. It was adopted whole. But every artefact it was judged on had no logo on it at all, because when they were made there was no logo to use. Its independence from the mark was demonstrated by the absence of marks.

The wordmark was finished on 2 August. This is the first time the two have been in the same frame, and it was the last of the five checks the wordmark owed.

The answer

They get along. The gate passes. Two surfaces were built — the same gig poster at the size it would print, and the image a shared link renders as.

But the predicted failure ran backwards. The worry written down in advance was that a heavy drawn logo would crowd a layout designed around empty space. What actually happens is the opposite: at the size the placeholder occupied, the real logo is too small. It has to roughly double.

NOT ARTWORK

What is real here and what is not

Read this before looking at anything below
  • The logo itself is finished artwork. Drawn, measured, and in the file set. That part is real and can be judged as final.
  • The two compositions are not. They are proofs built to test a rule, not approved layouts. Nobody has signed off on where anything sits.
  • Every word on the poster is placeholder. The wording came from round C as an example of the band’s voice, not as copy for a real event. 28.03.2027 is not a gig. It is Easter Sunday 2027, used because the anniversary falls there, and nothing is booked.
  • Both are renders. The poster is an A3 sheet rendered at print resolution — it has not been printed, and nothing in this project has been seen on paper, at physical size, or on a real phone.

Why the swap did not work at the same size

The placeholder was spaced out very wide and drawn very thin — a long pale line, the same kind of object as the activity strip above it. The real logo is narrow and solid. Put them at the same width and they still do not occupy the page the same way.

Matching the width does not match the presence. That is the sentence worth keeping, and it will apply to every other surface where the two meet.

This becomes a rule rather than a one-off judgement: where the logo shares a surface with the activity strip, it is drawn at at least 60% of that strip’s width. The strip sets the scale of the foot of the page; the logo has to answer it.

WHERE IT SITS

Three positions, and the one that keeps the hierarchy

THE LINK IMAGE

1200 × 630, and the one genuinely new problem

The second surface is the picture that appears when somebody shares an Apezone link — in a message, on social, in a search result. It is a real fixed size (1200 × 630) rather than a demonstration rectangle, which makes it the harder test: the logo is seven and a half times wider than it is tall, and that is a lot of horizontal commitment on a wide, short picture.

Why the link image has no writing on it

A link image is one fixed picture, and every app decides for itself how large to show it. The picture cannot respond. A website can drop an element when the screen gets narrow; a shared image cannot.

So anything small enough to sit as a caption at full size is a smudge in a chat preview. The rule that the dated logo only exists above a certain size — which a website can honour — is unenforceable on a picture like this. Hence: logo and strip, and nothing else.

And the worry that did not materialise

The concern written down in advance was that the logo’s width might force it below its minimum size on a picture this shape. It does not come close. The binding limit is a ceiling, not a floor — the logo can go up to 958 pixels before it runs out of the breathing space it is entitled to at the sides, against a minimum of 120. There is an eight-to-one range to work in.

SAYING THE NAME TWICE

A placement rule, not a rewrite

The line along the top of every surface begins with the word APEZONE. Put the real logo on the same page and the band’s name appears twice, in widely spaced capitals both times. That is worth taking seriously, and it turns out not to matter everywhere equally.

So this does not need the line rewritten. It needs a placement rule: the line does not sit next to the logo. Opposite ends of the surface, or one of them goes.

The shortened line (HELSINKI · 1997–2027) is off-spec and was built only so the trade could be looked at rather than argued about. If a future surface genuinely needs both elements adjacent, that is the fix, and it is Marcus’s call because the wording is currently fixed.

RULES, CHECKED BY COUNTING

Two of them round C's own poster broke

What the control turned up
  • Two warm accents where the rule allows one. The gold dash in the top line and the gold tick at the end of the activity strip. The rule that resolves it was written down back in the review — when the strip is present it takes the accent and the dash goes plain — and this rebuild is the first artefact anywhere to actually apply it.
  • Three lines set in a mid-grey that fails against the background. A contrast of 2.34 to 1, where 4.5 is the threshold for ordinary text. It looks fine on a good monitor and disappears in sunlight or on a cheap laptop screen.
And the consequence of fixing the second one

On the dark background there is exactly one legal colour for text, and it is the off-white. Everything else in the palette is either too faint, a background, or the accent — which is capped at 2% of any surface. So emphasis and recession have to come from size, weight, spacing and position alone.

That works — the small print on the poster still recedes — but it is a tighter constraint than round C was designed under, and it was not written down anywhere. It is now.

WHAT THIS CHANGES

Four documents, one question closed

The last check on the word — passed All five of the wordmark’s acceptance checks are now done. The word is finished.
The layout grammar keeps its adoption It was adopted on the strength of artefacts containing no logo. It has now been shown working with one, which is what the review said was still owed. Nothing about the grammar is reopened.
Two rules added to the build specification The logo is drawn at at least 60% of the activity strip’s width where they share a surface, and the top line never sits next to the logo. Both were invisible until the two elements were composed together.
One rule found to be unenforceable The dated logo’s minimum size cannot be honoured on a fixed share image, because the app picks the display size. Noted where the rule lives, with the workaround.
Still owed — the dark panel behind type on the shard background A separate question from this one, and still never demonstrated. Neither surface here uses the shard background, so nothing about it was proved. Building one to test it would have meant producing the real shard artwork, which is its own job.
METHOD AND LIMITS

What this cannot tell you

  • Nothing was printed. The poster is an A3 sheet rendered at 150 dots per inch, which is a picture of a print and not a print. The standing limit across this whole project is unchanged: nothing has been seen on paper, at physical size, or on a real phone.
  • The compositions are proofs, not approved layouts. Nobody has judged where the elements sit, only whether the rules survive them.
  • No skull on either surface, and nothing here tests one. That is deliberate, not a gap: the word is the one tier of the identity that has to stand entirely on its own, so a poster and a link image are exactly where it should be tested alone. Whatever state the skull is in, this gate says nothing about it either way.
  • The stamp was not composed onto either surface. It was rebuilt in the new typeface and works, but neither page had a job for it and forcing one in would have been decoration.
  • The columns-of-numbers setting is emulated, not switched on. The tool used to render these pages cannot enable the typeface’s own tabular-figures feature, so it was reproduced by hand — the same result, by a different route. The real feature is in the shipped font files.
  • One warm accent per surface was verified by counting connected regions, not by looking. Two separate gold shapes count as two even if one is tiny.
